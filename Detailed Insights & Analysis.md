# Insights Deep Dive

## Executive Overview

### 1. Support demand is increasing.

- Monthly tickets increased from **1,119 in RY24–25 to 1,199 in RY25–26**. Demand was also consistently higher during Mar–Jul than Nov–Feb.
- This indicates that support teams may need additional capacity during the higher-demand period.

### 2. Support performance has remained stable despite higher demand.

- Tickets increased, but **Resolution Rate improved 2.32% YoY** and overall CSAT remained stable.
- The support operation has therefore handled the higher workload without an overall decline in performance.

### 3. Resolution remains an important gap.

- Only **45.73% of tickets were resolved**. The remaining tickets ended as Refunded, Escalated, Pending, or Closed-No-Resolution.
- Improving successful ticket resolution remains an important opportunity for the support team.

### 4. Ticket outcome has a stronger relationship with CSAT than response speed.

- Resolved and Refunded tickets have CSAT around **4.4**, while Pending, Escalated, and Closed-No-Resolution tickets have much lower CSAT.
- FRT and RT show negligible relationships with CSAT, suggesting that achieving a successful outcome matters more than small improvements in response time.

### 5. Refunds create significant financial exposure.

- **24.44% of tickets resulted in refunds**, with Home Appliances and Air Coolers generating approximately **₹7.59M and ₹5.86M** in refund value.
- Refund activity is therefore an important area to monitor alongside support performance.

## Product Insights

### 1. Support growth is concentrated in a few categories.

- Kettles & Cookers grew **11.56%**, Air Coolers **7.17%**, and Kitchen Appliances **4.42% YoY**.
- This shows that the increase in support demand is mainly concentrated in specific categories.

### 2. Kettles & Cookers is the main emerging concern.

- Tickets increased **11.56%**, while CSAT declined **1.28%**. Around **29% of tickets were Product Defect-related**.
- Rising demand and lower satisfaction make this category a priority for further investigation.

### 3. High ticket volume does not necessarily mean poor performance.

- Kitchen Appliances had the highest workload at **4,859 tickets**, but its CSAT and Resolution Rate improved YoY.
- This shows that workload should be evaluated alongside customer and support outcomes.

### 4. Some products perform differently from their overall category.

- Easy Chef Food Processor had **4.62% higher tickets, 1.53% lower CSAT, and 4.76% lower Resolution Rate**.
- This shows why product-level analysis is important when category-level results look healthy.

### 5. Product Defect is a major source of support demand.

- Product Defect generated **6,155 tickets** and represented roughly **28–30% of tickets** in major categories such as Kettles & Cookers and Mixers & Grinders.
- Recurring product-quality issues are therefore an important contributor to support workload.

## Support Performance

### 1. Resolution is improving, but remains a gap.

- **45.73% of tickets were resolved**, although Resolution Rate improved **2.32% YoY**.
- Performance is improving, but fewer than half of tickets reaching resolution remains an important opportunity.

### 2. Poor ticket outcomes are associated with lower CSAT.

- CSAT is around **4.4 for Resolved tickets**, compared with **2.09 for Closed-No-Resolution, 3.03 for Pending, and 3.16 for Escalated**.
- Successful ticket outcomes are strongly associated with a better customer experience.

### 3. Faster response does not necessarily mean higher satisfaction.

- FRT–CSAT correlation is **-0.0345**, while RT–CSAT correlation is **-0.0285**. Both relationships are negligible.
- In this dataset, faster response or resolution alone does not appear to strongly improve CSAT.

### 4. Priority level has little difference in CSAT.

- CSAT is similar across Low, Medium, and High priority tickets, at roughly **3.89–3.92**.
- Priority level alone does not appear to be a major driver of customer satisfaction.

## Refund Insights

### 1. Refunds represent a significant financial exposure.

- **24.44% of all support tickets resulted in refunds**, making refunds a substantial part of post-purchase support outcomes.
- This highlights the importance of understanding which issues and products contribute most to refund activity.

### 2. Refund volume alone does not reflect financial exposure.

- Home Appliances generated **₹7.59M** in refunds, while Air Coolers generated **₹5.86M** despite having fewer refund tickets.
- This shows that categories with lower refund volumes can still account for substantial refund costs.

### 3. Air Coolers have a higher average refund value.

- Air Coolers averaged **₹9,263 per refunded ticket**, compared with **₹8,405 for Home Appliances**.
- Although Home Appliances generated more total refunds, each Air Cooler refund carried a higher average financial impact.

### 4. Product and fulfilment issues contribute to refund activity.

- Product Defect, Damaged in Transit, and Late Delivery contributed to both support workload and refund activity.
- These recurring issues connect operational problems with financial exposure, highlighting opportunities to reduce both support demand and refunds.

### 5. Spice Grinder Mini shows increasing refund pressure.

- Refund tickets for Spice Grinder Mini increased **36.94% YoY**, alongside high ticket volume and elevated escalation levels.
- The combination of rising refunds and existing support pressure makes this product a notable area of concern.

## Regional Analysis

### 1. Regional performance is broadly consistent, but pressure points differ.

- Resolution performance is relatively similar across the major regions, with South, East, Central and West all around the mid-40% range.

### 2. South shows relatively stronger overall outcomes.

- South handles a high ticket volume while maintaining good resolution performance and the lowest refund rate among the major regions.
- This makes South a useful benchmark when comparing regional performance.

### 3. North has the clearest closure bottleneck.

- North handles the highest workload, but also has a higher share of Closed-No-Resolution and Pending tickets.
- The region may benefit from investigating why more cases remain unresolved.

### 4. West and Central have different operational pressure points.

- West carries substantial support volume with relatively higher refunds, Pending cases and escalations.
- Central performs reasonably on resolution and refunds but has the highest escalation rate.

### 5. Regional performance requires targeted action.

- The regions show different strengths and weaknesses rather than one clearly underperforming region.
- Actions should therefore focus on the specific issue within each region rather than applying the same solution everywhere.

## Channel Analysis

### 1. CSAT is broadly consistent across channels.

- Channel-level CSAT ranges from **3.87 to 3.93**. Phone and Store have the highest CSAT at **3.93 and 3.92**, while Marketplace is lowest at **3.87**.

### 2. Marketplace has the clearest resolution concern.

- Marketplace has the lowest CSAT at **3.87** and the highest Closed-No-Resolution rate at **10.86%**.

### 3. Chat has higher refund activity.

- Chat has a relatively high workload of **4,517 tickets** and the highest refund rate at **25.59%**.
- This makes refund drivers within Chat worth investigating.

### 4. Channel performance varies by outcome.

- While overall CSAT is similar across channels, differences appear in resolution and refund outcomes.
- This suggests that each channel may need targeted improvements rather than a broad service-quality intervention.

## Agent Performance

### 1. Agent performance is relatively consistent.

- Resolution rates range from approximately **42%–50%**, showing moderate variation across the team.
- No large gap exists between agents, but there is still room to improve overall resolution performance.

### 2. Some agents show stronger overall performance.

- Agents 12, 19 and 1 show a stronger balance across resolution, response time, CSAT, and escalation/refund outcomes.
- Their performance can provide useful benchmarks for the wider team.

### 3. Agent 9 stands out for handling speed.

- Agent 9 shows particularly strong response and resolution speed.
- This makes the agent useful for identifying practices that may help improve handling efficiency.
